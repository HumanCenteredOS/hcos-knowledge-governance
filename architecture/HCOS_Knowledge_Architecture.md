# HCOS™ Knowledge Architecture

## Purpose

The HCOS™ Knowledge Architecture defines how knowledge assets are differentiated, related, and organized across the ecosystem.

## Knowledge Categories

### Foundations

Enduring principles describing what HCOS believes about healthy human systems.

### Disciplines

Major domains in which HCOS knowledge is developed and applied.

### Standards

Expectations describing what responsible and human-centered practice should look like.

### Methods

Repeatable approaches for performing work.

### Instruments

Practical tools that help people apply or evaluate Methods, Standards, and principles.

### Research

Evidence used to examine, validate, refine, or challenge HCOS concepts.

### Insights

Emerging observations and interpretations that may reveal broader systems lessons.

### Learning Resources

Educational materials that help people understand and apply HCOS.

### Examples

Worked applications that demonstrate use without independently establishing authority.

### Governance

Policies and processes that define how HCOS decisions are made and maintained.

### Stewardship

Practices that sustain, improve, and preserve the ecosystem over time.

## Typical Relationship

```text
Foundations → Disciplines → Standards → Methods → Instruments
                        ↘ Research, Insights, Learning Resources, Examples
```

Governance and Stewardship support every category.

## Classification Principle

Classification should reflect the artifact's primary function, not merely its format. A prompt may be an Instrument. A long paper may still be an Insight. A checklist may implement a Standard without itself becoming one.

## Dependency Principle

Documents should identify important dependencies and downstream impacts. A Method may depend on a Standard. An Instrument may implement a Method. A Learning Resource may explain several related artifacts.

## Architectural Integrity

New work should strengthen the ecosystem rather than create isolated fragments. Contributors should consider whether an existing document should be revised before creating a new one.
