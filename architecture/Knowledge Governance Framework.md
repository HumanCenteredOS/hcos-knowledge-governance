# HCOS™ Knowledge Governance Framework

## Purpose

The HCOS™ Knowledge Governance Framework defines how the HCOS ecosystem learns, evaluates evidence, organizes knowledge, makes architectural decisions, and sustains its body of work over time.

It serves as the bridge among three complementary architectures:

1. Knowledge Architecture — what HCOS knows.
2. Governance Architecture — how decisions about HCOS are made.
3. Stewardship Architecture — how HCOS remains healthy over time.

## Core Principle

> Healthy knowledge ecosystems do not merely collect information. They preserve meaning, make reasoning visible, govern change responsibly, and steward learning in service of human flourishing.

## Objectives

The framework is designed to:

- preserve architectural coherence
- distinguish evidence maturity from document popularity
- create traceable pathways from observation to principle
- align authority with responsibility
- prevent unnecessary duplication and fragmentation
- preserve uncertainty, limitations, and dissent
- support continuous learning and responsible revision

## Governance Domains

### Knowledge Classification

Determines whether work is a Foundation, Discipline, Standard, Method, Instrument, Research, Insight, Learning Resource, Example, Governance artifact, or Stewardship artifact.

### Evidence Governance

Makes provenance, maturity, uncertainty, and evidentiary limitations visible.

### Decision Governance

Documents significant architectural choices, alternatives, rationale, affected artifacts, and review dates.

### Document Governance

Controls metadata, lifecycle status, versioning, review, release, supersession, and archival treatment.

### Relationship Governance

Maintains dependencies and cross-references among Foundations, Disciplines, Standards, Methods, Instruments, and supporting resources.

## Governance and Stewardship

Governance establishes how decisions are made. Stewardship cares for the consequences of those decisions over time.

Governance without Stewardship may become rigid. Stewardship without Governance may become inconsistent. HCOS requires both.

## Proportionality

Governance depth should reflect potential consequence. A spelling correction does not require the same process as a new Foundation, a high-impact AI Standard, or a change affecting multiple repositories.

## Human-Centered Review

Significant decisions should ask:

- Whose dignity, safety, agency, work, or access may be affected?
- Whose perspective is missing?
- What evidence supports the decision?
- What assumptions and uncertainty remain?
- Could the process or artifact be misused?
- How will learning be captured after implementation?

## Relationship to Supporting Documents

This framework is implemented through the policies, standards, instruments, templates, and stewardship processes contained in this repository.

## Vision

HCOS should be capable of learning without losing coherence, changing without hiding its reasoning, and growing without forgetting the people its architecture exists to serve.
