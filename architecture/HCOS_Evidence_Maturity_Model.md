# HCOS™ Evidence Maturity Model

## Purpose

The HCOS™ Evidence Maturity Model provides a transparent path from an initial observation to an enduring architectural principle.

## Maturity Levels

| Level | Name | Description | Typical Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Observation | A single source, event, case, or experience suggests a lesson. | Insight |
| 2 | Corroborated Observation | Multiple independent sources reveal a similar concern or pattern. | Expanded Insight or research question |
| 3 | Emerging Pattern | Repetition suggests a broader systems dynamic. | Synthesis topic, exploratory Method, or Instrument |
| 4 | Developing Principle | Evidence across sources or contexts supports a durable lesson. | Candidate Standard, Method, or governance guidance |
| 5 | Established Principle | The principle is supported across contexts, disciplines, implementation, or time. | Mature Standard or Discipline principle |
| 6 | Foundational Principle | The principle appears enduring across technologies and historical periods. | Candidate Foundation |

## Evaluation Dimensions

Assess:

- independence of sources
- diversity of settings and disciplines
- quality and relevance of evidence
- consistency across time
- known contradictions
- implementation experience
- human consequences
- transferability beyond current technology

## Evidence Types

Appropriate evidence may include:

- empirical research
- operational evidence
- lived experience
- professional judgment
- qualitative findings
- stakeholder testimony
- historical evidence
- ethical reasoning
- implementation learning

Evidence types should not be treated as interchangeable. Their provenance, strengths, and limitations should remain visible.

## Maturity Is Not Certainty

Maturity reflects the strength and breadth of support, not infallibility. Established and Foundational principles remain open to correction when new evidence or previously excluded perspectives emerge.
