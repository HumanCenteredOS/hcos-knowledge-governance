# HCOS™ Knowledge Lifecycle

## Purpose

The HCOS™ Knowledge Lifecycle describes how evidence, experience, and emerging ideas move through discovery, evaluation, publication, and long-term stewardship.

## Lifecycle

```text
External Evidence or Experience
              ↓
          Observation
              ↓
           Discovery
              ↓
        Classification
              ↓
           Synthesis
              ↓
           Evolution
              ↓
       Governance Review
              ↓
          Publication
              ↓
       Stewardship Review
              ↓
Revision, Expansion, Supersession, or Retirement
```

## Stages

### Observation

A source, event, experience, or implementation reveals a possible lesson.

### Discovery

The enduring systems, organizational, architectural, and human lessons are interpreted.

### Classification

The work is placed in the appropriate HCOS category and repository.

### Synthesis

Multiple observations are considered collectively to identify recurring patterns, contradictions, and candidate principles.

### Evolution

Accumulated evidence is translated into proposed additions or revisions to HCOS architecture.

### Governance Review

The proposal is evaluated for evidence, coherence, impact, proportionality, and human consequence.

### Publication

Approved work is versioned, released, linked, and communicated.

### Stewardship Review

The artifact is monitored for usefulness, alignment, evidence changes, implementation learning, and maintenance needs.

## Proportional Use

Not every artifact must pass through every stage. Insights may remain Insights. Minor corrections may move directly through lightweight review. Foundational changes require deeper synthesis and governance.

## Lifecycle Outcomes

An artifact may be:

- maintained
- revised
- expanded
- merged
- superseded
- deprecated
- archived
