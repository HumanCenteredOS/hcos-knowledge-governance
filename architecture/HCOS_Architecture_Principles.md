# HCOS™ Architecture Principles

## Purpose

These principles define the qualities expected of the HCOS™ ecosystem architecture.

## Principles

### Human-Centeredness

Architectural choices should support dignity, agency, understanding, safety, participation, and flourishing.

### Coherence

Components should fit together logically and avoid unnecessary contradiction or duplication.

### Traceability

Important claims, decisions, and revisions should be connected to evidence and rationale.

### Transparency

Assumptions, uncertainty, limitations, dissent, and decision authority should remain visible.

### Proportionality

Governance and documentation effort should correspond to potential consequence.

### Extensibility

New knowledge should be incorporable without destabilizing the wider architecture.

### Stewardability

Documents, relationships, and repositories should remain understandable and maintainable over time.

### Evidence Maturity

The architectural authority of an idea should reflect the maturity and breadth of its support.

### Relationship Awareness

Artifacts should identify dependencies and downstream consequences.

### Learning

The architecture should capture implementation experience and support responsible revision.

### Accessibility

Knowledge should be understandable to intended audiences without erasing necessary complexity.

### Anti-Fragility Through Humility

The ecosystem should remain capable of correction. No document should be protected from evidence merely because it is established.

## Guiding Test

> Does this architectural choice help HCOS become more coherent, trustworthy, learnable, maintainable, and capable of serving people over time?
