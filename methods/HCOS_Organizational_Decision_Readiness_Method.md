# HCOS™ Organizational Decision Readiness Method

**Category:** Method  
**Status:** Draft  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Date:** July 31, 2026  
**Implements:** HCOS™ Organizational AI Readiness Standard

## Purpose

This Method provides a repeatable, human-centered process for determining whether an organization understands a proposed AI intervention and its surrounding human system well enough to make a responsible decision.

> Are we prepared to decide whether and how this defined AI capability should enter this defined workflow for this defined purpose?

The Method does not assume implementation should occur.

## Participants

A credible review should include appropriate representation from accountable leadership, workflow ownership, frontline participants, technical expertise, governance functions, evidence stewardship, implementation support, and affected people or their representatives.

## Evidence Sources

Evidence may include interviews, observation, workflow maps, policies, metrics, incident reports, workload data, financial information, user feedback, system logs, research, vendor documentation, risk reviews, implementation history, and lived experience.

Observed evidence, interpretation, and recommendation should remain distinguishable.

# Phase 1 — Frame the Decision

## Objective

Clarify the actual decision before evaluating the technology.

## Activities

- define the problem;
- identify who experiences it;
- document evidence that it exists;
- specify the proposed capability;
- define workflow and population;
- identify alternatives;
- examine the consequence of no action;
- confirm that the decision is genuinely open.

## Outputs

- Decision Statement
- Problem Statement
- Alternative Set

## Gate

Do not proceed if the problem, scope, purpose, or decision owner cannot be defined.

# Phase 2 — Understand the Current Human System

## Objective

Develop a defensible picture of how work actually occurs.

## Review Areas

Workflow, information, roles, decisions, handoffs, dependencies, policies, metrics, resources, workload, exceptions, workarounds, compensatory labor, recovery time, and affected relationships.

## Outputs

- Current-State System Map
- System Friction Summary
- HCOS Eight System Forces Review

## Gate

Do not automate a step until the organization understands why it exists and what hidden functions people perform within it.

# Phase 3 — Map the Proposed Change and Consequences

## Objective

Determine what will change if the capability is introduced.

## Activities

- identify work removed, retained, and created;
- map shifts in authority;
- define monitoring and exception work;
- identify correction responsibilities;
- assess downtime and reversibility;
- identify people who may be overlooked;
- examine whether dysfunction could be accelerated.

## Outputs

- Future-State Consequence Map
- Human-System Impact Map
- Three Protections and Meaning Anchor Review

## Gate

Pause when material consequences cannot be assigned to an accountable owner or depend on unspecified human compensation.

# Phase 4 — Review Governance, Authority, and Accountability

## Objective

Establish how the capability will be bounded and governed.

## Review Areas

Accountability, permissible and prohibited uses, human review, decision rights, approval, incident response, escalation, auditability, records, access, change control, pause, withdrawal, appeal, correction, and reassessment.

## Outputs

- Decision Ownership and Authority Map
- Governance Boundary Statement
- Escalation and Correction Pathway

## Gate

Do not proceed when accountability is diffuse, review is impossible, or unsafe use cannot be stopped.

# Phase 5 — Evaluate Evidence, Value, and Uncertainty

## Objective

Determine whether expected value and risks are sufficiently understood.

## Review Areas

Baseline, capability evidence, workflow fit, benefits, direct and indirect costs, burden, risk, human effects, uncertainty, assumptions, conflicts, vendor claims, and generalizability.

## Outputs

- Evidence Summary
- Theory of Value
- Decision Uncertainty Map

For each uncertainty, document what is unknown, why it matters, ownership, whether it can be resolved before implementation, and how it will be monitored.

## Gate

Do not scale when expected value cannot be distinguished from marketing claims or success cannot be detected.

# Phase 6 — Assess Capacity and Implementation Readiness

## Objective

Determine whether the organization can execute and sustain the change.

## Review Areas

Staffing, time, ownership, training, communication, support, integration, testing, workflow redesign, continuity, exceptions, finances, competing priorities, and recovery capacity.

## Outputs

- Capacity and Resource Plan
- Implementation Dependency Map
- Readiness Gaps

## Gate

Do not equate executive approval or deployment capability with readiness.

# Phase 7 — Determine the Decision Path

## Outcomes

1. Not sufficiently examined
2. Gather additional evidence
3. Redesign the surrounding system first
4. Pilot under bounded conditions
5. Proceed with required protections
6. Ready for responsible implementation
7. Do not proceed under current conditions

## Outputs

- Decision Readiness Profile
- Decision-Critical Findings
- Recommended Decision Path
- Required Protections

# Phase 8 — Establish Learning and Reassessment

## Objective

Keep implementation reviewable, correctable, and reversible.

## Activities

Define outcomes, human-impact measures, failure signals, review ownership, cadence, escalation triggers, pause criteria, withdrawal criteria, scaling evidence, and reassessment timing.

## Outputs

- Learning Plan
- Reassessment Schedule
- Pause and Withdrawal Criteria

## Six Primary Method Outputs

1. Decision Readiness Profile
2. Decision Uncertainty Map
3. Human-System Impact Map
4. Readiness Gaps and Required Protections
5. Recommended Decision Path
6. Learning and Reassessment Plan

## Interpretation Principles

- Readiness is use-case-specific.
- Uncertainty should be governed, not hidden.
- A profile is more meaningful than one score.
- Critical gaps may override stronger findings elsewhere.
- Low readiness is not a moral judgment.
- Resistance may reveal systems evidence.
- Human impact must not be subordinated to technical performance.
- A pilot is a learning mechanism, not a substitute for governance.
- Proceeding is not the default outcome.
- Evidence, interpretation, and recommendation should remain distinct.

## Proportional Application

- **Lightweight review:** low-consequence, reversible, bounded uses.
- **Structured review:** meaningful workflow or human effects.
- **Comprehensive review:** high-consequence decisions, agentic behavior, authority shifts, sensitive populations, or difficult-to-reverse change.

## Limitations

This Method does not eliminate uncertainty, validate a model, establish legal compliance, or predict financial return. It depends on evidence quality and may require trained facilitation.

Until validated, it should be described as an evidence-informed decision-support Method.
